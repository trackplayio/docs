Your audience data is yours. TrackPlay exports it in two shapes, and the difference
between them matters: one is safe to upload straight to an ad platform, the other
contains contact information and needs to stay inside your team.

## The two formats

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Customer match">
    A two-column CSV: `email`, `phone`, both **SHA-256 hashed**. Nothing else.

    Every value is normalized the same way ad platforms expect: lowercase, trimmed, then
    hashed. That is deliberate. Meta, Google, and TikTok all hash a customer list the same
    way before matching it, so a hash TrackPlay produces from `Jane@Acme.com` matches the
    hash the ad platform produces from the same address on its side. Upload it directly as
    a Custom Audience or Customer Match list.

    <Warning>
      No raw email address and no raw phone number leave your app in this format. If a
      value did not already have a hash on file, TrackPlay hashes the raw contact at
      export time rather than including it plain, so the file that downloads is always
      hash-only.
    </Warning>

    Rows with neither an email hash nor a phone hash, and exact duplicate hash pairs, are
    dropped before the file downloads. There is no reason to upload noise to an ad
    platform.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Follow-up sheet">
    The full picture: raw contact (email, name, phone, wherever a conversion or a form
    captured one), ad-platform click ids for manual lookups, traffic source and landing
    page, and any custom events this person triggered.

    This format carries real PII. Build the file for your sales or support team to work a
    list, not to upload anywhere.

    <Warning>
      The follow-up sheet includes raw email addresses and phone numbers where TrackPlay
      has them. Treat the downloaded file the way you would treat any customer contact
      list: do not paste it into a tool that was not built to hold PII.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Two independent choices

Every export is one request with two parameters, chosen independently.

| Parameter | Values | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scope` | `all` or `hot` | `all` exports every identified viewer (one row per profile). `hot` exports the current Hot Leads list (one row per session). |
| `format` | `customer_match` or `full` | Which of the two shapes above. `customer_match` is the default. |

So `scope=hot&format=full` is your call list with real phone numbers and emails attached.
`scope=all&format=customer_match` is every viewer you have ever identified, hashed and
ready to upload as a retargeting audience.

## How the export runs

<Warning>
  This is a **direct download**, not a background job. The file streams to you the moment
  you request it. There is no queue, no email with a link, and no waiting.
</Warning>

That directness comes with hard limits, so one export request can never turn into an
unbounded database scan:

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Max rows, `scope=all` | 5,000 profiles |
| Max rows, `scope=hot` | 500 leads |
| Rate limit | 10 requests per minute |

If your audience is larger than the cap, export in the scope that fits your need (`hot`
is almost always what you want for follow-up) rather than expecting a second file to pick
up where the first left off. There is no pagination on this endpoint today.

<Note>
  This is different from the video-report export in the API, which does run as an
  asynchronous job and hands back a download URL for larger jobs. Audience exports and
  video-report exports are separate mechanisms with separate limits. Do not assume the
  async behavior of one applies to the other.
</Note>
